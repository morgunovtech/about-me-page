The essential kit

Apps that close the main gaps: messages, mail, passwords, network and files. Each one passed two filters:

  • open source — anyone qualified can check what the program really does instead of taking it on trust;
  • independent audit — an outside security team examined the product and published a report. The link to the report is in the app’s row.

One exception sits at the end and is marked as such: an ad blocker — none of them has been audited.

An audit is a snapshot of one date, not a lifetime guarantee. Keep the apps updated and download them only from the official site — its address is in the “Site” line.

Communication

Signal (opens in a new tab)

Messenger · Free

The standard for private messaging: chats, groups and calls are always end-to-end encrypted, and the server knows almost nothing about who talks to whom. Needs a phone number. The protocol has been verified; the app code has had no public audit.

Email · Free plan

Network

Mullvad VPN (opens in a new tab)

VPN · €5 / month

Hides your IP address from websites and your traffic from your internet provider and public Wi-Fi. No e-mail or phone needed to sign up: the account is just a number. The apps and servers are audited regularly. Sweden; there is no free plan.

modDNS (opens in a new tab)

DNS · In IVPN Plus — $8 / month

Encrypts your DNS queries — they show which sites you open — so your internet provider and public Wi-Fi cannot read them. It also blocks ads and trackers by blocklists in every app at once, not only in the browser. The whole server is open source. Needs an IVPN account; the audit covered security, not a no-logs promise.

Tor Browser (opens in a new tab)

Browser · Free

Hides your IP address and makes all its users look alike, so websites cannot recognise you by your browser fingerprint. Slower than a regular browser — keep it for what is really worth hiding. Where Tor is blocked, connect through bridges, which are built in.

Tails (opens in a new tab)

Operating system · Free

An operating system on a USB stick: boot a computer from it, all traffic goes through Tor, and nothing is left behind when you shut down. For when more is at stake than ads. It does not run on Macs with Apple chips.

Open source, no audit yet

No browser ad blocker has a public audit. This is the most trustworthy of the open-source ones. To block ads in every app at once, use modDNS from the “Network” group — its audit is published.

uBlock Origin (opens in a new tab)

Ad blocker · Free

Blocks ads and trackers in the browser. A single author has maintained it since 2015, with no monetisation, and Mozilla lists it as Recommended. No independent audit. Firefox gets the full version; Safari and Chrome get uBlock Origin Lite, which is weaker against sites that fight ad blocking.
